# Scotch distillery pronunciations

Date: 2026-10-21

## Scope

This pass filled the Entity `pronunciation` and `pronunciationIpa` fields for production Entities with kind `distillery` and country Scotland. It started with 294 such records, none of which had a pronunciation. 178 records are real Scottish whisky production sites (operating, closed, demolished, or mothballed). The other 116 are bottlers, merchants, brands, anonymous label wording, non-Scottish producers, or junk records stored with the wrong kind. They were left unchanged and are listed below for a separate kind review.

123 production sites were written: 107 in the first pass, 13 in a second pass, and 3 from the owner. 55 were left unknown: 10 because credible sources still conflict and 45 because no acceptable source was found.

## Sources

- English Wikipedia distillery articles that show IPA or a respelling.
- English Wikipedia and Wiktionary entries for the exact place or word the distillery is named for.
- Producer pages that state a pronunciation, such as Diageo's [malts.com](https://www.malts.com) FAQs, [The Glenlivet](https://www.theglenlivet.com) FAQ, and [Lochlea](https://lochleadistillery.com).
- Published whisky pronunciation guides: Summerton Whisky Club, Difford's Guide, The Spirits Business, Whisky for Everyone, The Whiskey Wash, and [Whiskipedia](https://whiskipedia.com/distillery-names/).

Several guides could not be read here: Wine Enthusiast, The Scotsman, whisky.com, Collins, and the Master of Malt blog returned 403 or 429. The University of Edinburgh whisky guide gives pronunciations as audio only. Unsourced names may be in those guides.

## Decisions

- The owner required a cited source for every written value, including plain-English names.
- **Strict** values (47) come from one credible source that gives the whole name with its stress: a Wikipedia distillery article, a Wikipedia or Wiktionary entry for the exact place name, the producer, or a guide that marks stress.
- **Borderline** values (60) were written with the owner's approval because no source conflicts. They have weaker evidence: stress from Whiskipedia alone (a hobby site whose capitals appear to mark stress), stress inferred where a source gives only the sounds, sounds and stress from different sources, or names assembled from separate dictionary and place-name entries (for example Highland Park, Port Ellen, Isle of Jura, and `X Distillery` names). Prefer a stronger source when one appears.
- When a source gave only a respelling, the IPA was transcribed from that respelling. When a source gave only IPA, the respelling was written from that IPA.
- A leading "The" was dropped when the sources drop it (The Macallan, The Balvenie, The Glenlivet).
- Values copied from Wikipedia IPA keep its symbols, including rhotic `r` and `oʊ`, so they are not all in one accent.
- After the first pass, the owner allowed any source that the operator checks directly. Every written value's source page was re-read to confirm the quoted text and stress mark. That check changed three values: Highland Park and Royal Lochnagar now follow the Summerton guide, which gives the whole name, instead of values assembled from dictionary entries. Glen Garioch now uses "GEER-ee", which the Garioch place article and the Summerton guide give, instead of the distillery article's "GEE-ree".
- Aberlour keeps the Wikipedia distillery article's stress on the last syllable, which Whiskipedia also gives. The Summerton guide stresses the first syllable.
- A conflict was settled only when at least two independent checked sources agree on the whole name and stress, and the disagreeing source is not the producer. Pittyvaich follows The Spirits Business and Whisky for Everyone over Difford's Guide. Pulteney follows the Summerton guide and Whiskipedia.
- The Glenlivet FAQ and the Whisky for Everyone guide returned 403 during the check. The Glenlivet's stress is confirmed by the Summerton guide. Glenugie rests on Whisky for Everyone alone.
- Girvan, Glengoyne, Glengyle, Glendullan, Glen Ord, and Glen Spey use stress that the operator inferred because the source gives no stress. The owner approved these as borderline values.
- The owner settled three split names directly: Caol Ila "cull-EE-luh", Ardbeg "ARD-beg", and Clynelish "KLINE-leesh". Each agrees with at least one checked guide.
- Each update also queued the Entity's normal derived jobs, including generated details for empty fields. The owner accepted that.
