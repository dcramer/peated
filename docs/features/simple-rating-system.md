# Community Rating Systems

## Overview

Peated offers two separate ways to rate a tasting:

- **Simple**: Pass, Sip, or Savor for a quick, intentional reaction.
- **100-point**: A whole-number score for tasters who want more precision.

Simple remains the default. Users can choose 100-point ratings as their account
preference. Tasting forms always show the preferred system and do not include a
system selector. A tasting can use one system or neither, but never both.

## Pass, Sip, or Savor

### Pass

**Not my thing.** The user would decline it in the future.

### Sip

**Enjoyable; would drink again.** A positive experience worth repeating.

### Savor

**Amazing; would seek out.** A memorable whisky the user would pursue.

Bottle pages present simple ratings as a distribution. Peated does not convert
them into 100-point scores.

## The 100-Point Scale

The advanced scale gives experienced users more room to compare whiskies over
time. Scores are whole numbers from 0 through 100.

| Range  | Meaning                                       |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| 95-100 | Extraordinary; among the best you have tasted |
| 90-94  | Exceptional; distinctive and memorable        |
| 85-89  | Very good; strongly recommended               |
| 80-84  | Good; solid and enjoyable                     |
| 75-79  | Fair; drinkable but ordinary or flawed        |
| 0-74   | Not recommended                               |

An 80 means good; it is not a school grade. Scores of 90 and above should be
rare, and 95 and above should represent extraordinary experiences. One point
can express a small personal preference, but it is not scientific precision.

To choose a score, taste first and use 80 as the starting point for a good,
enjoyable whisky with no major problem. Move up for clear flavors, balance,
depth, a pleasant texture, and a finish that lasts. Move down for off flavors,
rough alcohol, thin texture, poor balance, or a finish that fades quickly.

Once the whisky is in a band, use the bottom when it just fits, the middle when
it clearly fits, and the top when it nearly reaches the next band. For example,
85 just enters Very good, 87 is clearly Very good, and 89 nearly reaches
Exceptional. This is one judgment of the whole whisky, not a sum of required
subscores.

Price, rarity, packaging, and reputation are excluded so a score remains useful
when market circumstances change.

## Recording and Editing

New accounts default to Simple. The Ratings section of account settings can
change the preferred system to 100-point.

When editing a tasting, Peated opens the system selected in profile settings.
If the tasting uses the other system, Peated keeps its rating until the user
enters a new rating. The new rating replaces the previous rating. Peated never
converts between systems.

A user can clear a rating entirely. Notes, flavor tags, serving style, and other
tasting context do not require a rating.

## Community Scores on Bottles

Peated shows advanced community scores as an arithmetic average with one
decimal place and the number of contributing scores. Simple distributions and
advanced averages appear separately.

Every exact batch, pick, single cask, or other marketed release is a Bottle.
Its scores contribute to that exact Bottle and to the BottleGroup that relates
releases of the same expression. A sibling release's exact score remains
independent.

Peated does not currently trim outliers or weight experienced reviewers more
heavily. Sparse averages always include their count and should not be treated as
a confident ranking.

## Critic Reviews

Scores under **The Critics** come from the named publication. When a source's
permitted native score uses 100 points, Peated adds the same band labels to make
it scannable. Each publication owns its review methodology.

Critic reviews never contribute to community averages. Peated also does not
combine community 100-point scores with Pass/Sip/Savor or historical five-star
ratings.

## Public Explanation

The user-facing methodology lives at `/about/ratings`. Advanced form guidance and
community score summaries link to that page so the scale is available at the
moment a user assigns or interprets a score.

## Historical Ratings

Peated previously used a five-star scale. Those values remain preserved as
legacy data for provenance. They are not converted into either current system
and do not affect current community aggregates.
